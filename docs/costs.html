Jigoku Card builder reference

Costs and limits

Every cost an ability can have, what it records, and the limits on how often an ability can be used.

Costs

A cost is what the player pays to use an ability: the imperative instructions before the dash ("Bow this character", "Lose 1 honor", "Sacrifice a character"). Call .cost(...) once per cost; all costs of an ability are paid together.

Import the costs as a namespace: import * as costs from '../../costs/index.js'. Many costs share a name with a game action (bow, dishonor, …); the namespace keeps them apart. The functions live in boardCosts.ts, fateAndHonorCosts.ts and variableAndOptionalCosts.ts.

Any imperative instruction (other than "choose…," which denotes one or more targets must be chosen, or "select…," which denotes that one or more of a number of effects must be selected) that appears before the dash of a triggered ability is considered a part of that ability’s cost.
Cost
If multiple costs for a single card or ability require payment, those costs must be paid simultaneously. If any part of a cost payment is prevented, once all costs that can be paid are paid, the process of initiating the ability or playing the card immediately ends without further resolution.
Cost
If the effects of an ability are canceled, the ability is still considered to have been used, and any costs have still been paid.

// Assassination: Conflict Action: Lose 3 honor. Choose a character with printed cost 2 or lower – discard that character. (Max 1 per round.)
this.conflictAction('Discard a character')
    .cost(costs.payHonor(3))
    .target({
        cardType: CardType.Character,
        cardCondition: (card) => card.costLessThan(3)
    }, discardFromPlay())
    .max(perRound(1));

How the engine pays

  1. Legality. Before the ability can be used, the engine asks every cost canPay(context) on a copy of the context at Stage.Cost. If one says no, the ability is not offered (blocker CannotPayCost). Nothing is chosen or paid yet, so context.costs is empty.
  2. Early targets. Unless the ability has cannotTargetFirst(), its targets are checked and offered first (stage PreTarget); the player may pick "Pay costs first". The targets are confirmed after the costs.
  3. Choosing. The costs resolve in the order they were declared: each one asks its question (which card, how much fate, Yes or No) and collects the events that pay it. While the resolver allows it, every prompt has a Cancel button; cancelling ends the ability and nothing is paid.
  4. Paying. All collected events resolve in one event window, so the costs are paid simultaneously. If any of them is cancelled or prevented, the chat says "player1 attempted to use X, but did not successfully pay the required costs" and the ability ends; what was paid stays paid.
  5. Then the targets are chosen or confirmed. If no legal target is left, the chat says "player1 attempted to use X, but there are insufficient legal targets" and the ability ends; the costs stay paid. Otherwise the limit and max count the use, the chat line is written and the effects initiate. The effects can still be cancelled; the costs stay paid.
  • An event card gets payReduceableFateCost() (its printed fate cost) added after its own costs automatically; don't declare it.
  • Effects can add costs: additionalTriggerCost (on the player), additionalTriggerCostForCard (on the source card) and additionalPlayCost (on the player). They are paid with the ability's own costs, but not by a sub-resolution (an ability resolving again).
  • An ability resolved by another card's effect (resolveAbility) pays its own costs without a Cancel button. It skips the printed fate cost and the added costs. With Blocker.CannotPayCost among its ignored blockers, it pays no costs at all.
  • Costs are checked and paid at Stage.Cost. A restriction scoped to card effects (RestrictionScope.CardEffects, and the scopes for one player's card effects) doesn't stop a cost. One scoped to RestrictionScope.LoseHonorAsCost stops only costs.
  • Choosing a card for a cost is not targeting it: "cannot be targeted" doesn't protect it and no targeting cost is paid.
  • A then() step can declare its own costs; its context.costs starts with a copy of what the ability recorded.

What costs record

Once paid, a cost may record what was paid in context.costs, typed by the builder (your editor shows the keys). A selection cost records the chosen card under its own name, and a snapshot of it under <name>StateWhenChosen: costs.dishonor(…) gives context.costs.dishonor and context.costs.dishonorStateWhenChosen. Read the snapshot when the card changes while paying (a sacrificed card has left play; the snapshot still has its traits and skill).

A multi-card mode (Exactly, UpTo, Unlimited, …) records a list, even for one card, and no snapshot: the StateWhenChosen key is typed but stays undefined.

The costs on the source card and the fixed fate and honor costs also write the paid targets under their game action's name (bowSelf() writes context.costs.bow, a list holding the source), but their result type is empty, so the builder types those keys as unknown. Don't rely on them; read context.source instead.

context.costs is only set once the costs are paid. Conditions, target conditions and canPay checks also run before that, with context.costs empty: handle undefined (Expert Bartering treats "not paid yet" as paid: context.costs.optionalFatePaid === undefined || context.costs.optionalFatePaid > 0). When a target depends on what a cost recorded, add .cannotTargetFirst() so the target is chosen after the costs (Bonds of Blood, Sanguine Mastery, Rebuild).

Chat text

The chat line of an ability lists the cost texts between the card and the effect: player1 uses Asahina Artisan, bowing Asahina Artisan to give Doji Whisperer +3 political skill. Each entry below gives its cost text. In it, {0} is what the cost recorded (the paid card or cards; a facedown card reads "a facedown card") and {1}, {2} are the cost's own values. A cost without a cost text adds nothing to the line; a few print a line of their own while paying.

// cards/captureCost.ts: a free cost that records a value before later costs change it (Niten records its parent before returning to hand)
export function captureCost<Name extends string, Value>(name: Name, capture: (context: AbilityContext) => Value): Cost<{ [Key in Name]: Value }> {
    return {
        canPay() {
            return true;
        },
        resolve(context) {
            Object.assign(context.costs, { [name]: capture(context) });
        },
        pay() {}
    };
}

Paying for the source card

These costs act on the card with the ability (or its parent) and take no choice. They can't be paid when that card can't be affected: it is in the wrong place, already in that state, or a restriction forbids it. They write the paid card under the game action's name in context.costs (untyped, see above).

bowParent() Cost #

"Bow attached character": bows the character the source is attached to.

  • Can't be paid when the source has no parent, or the parent is not in play, is already bowed or can't be bowed.
  • Chat: bowing {0}, with the parent as {0}.
// Tattered Missive: Action: Bow attached character – search the top 5 cards of your conflict deck for a card, reveal it, and add it to your hand. Shuffle.
this.action('Search top 5 cards')
    .condition((context) => context.player.conflictDeck.length > 0)
    .cost(costs.bowParent())
    .deckSearch({ … });

Used by Tattered Missive, Being and Becoming

bowSelf() Cost #

"Bow this character / this card": bows the source.

  • Can't be paid when the source is already bowed, is not in the play area, or a restriction stops the bow. A stronghold is the exception to the location check: it can be bowed from the stronghold province.
  • Only characters, attachments and strongholds can be bowed; on a holding or province this cost can never be paid.
  • Chat: bowing {0}.
// Paranoid Hōsōshi: Action: During the conflict phase, bow this character. Choose a character with the highest cost (you choose in case of a tie) - move 1 fate from that character to your fate pool.
this.action('Steal fate from a character')
    .phase(Phase.Conflict)
    .cost(costs.bowSelf())
    .target({ … }, removeFate((context) => ({ recipient: context.player })));

Used by Paranoid Hōsōshi, Asahina Artisan, City of the Open Hand, Daimyō's Favor

putSelfIntoPlay() Cost #

"Put this character into play": puts the source into play for the ability's player, at home, ordinary, with no fate.

  • Can't be paid when the source is not a character, is already in play or facedown, another copy of a unique character is in play for the player, or a restriction stops it from entering play.

returnSelfToHand() Cost #

"Return this card to your hand": returns the source from play to its owner's hand.

  • Can't be paid when the source is not in play, is not a character, attachment or event, or can't be returned to hand.
  • The source leaves play: its own attachments are discarded and its fate removed, and it is no longer attached to anything when the effects resolve. Record what you need first with a cost declared before it (Niten records its parent with captureParentCost()).

    .cost(costs.returnSelfToHand())

switchLocation() Cost #

"Move this character home or to the conflict": sends the source home when it is participating, otherwise moves it into the conflict. The player is not asked; where the character is decides.

  • Records switchLocation: the source.
  • Chat: moving {1} home or moving {1} to the conflict, with the source as {1}.

Choosing a card to pay with

These costs ask the player to choose a card, then pay with it ("Bow a Spirit character you control", "Sacrifice a participating Berserker character"). They take the properties of the selectCard game action, minus gameAction. Unlike a target(), there is no dependsOn, optional or hideIfNoLegalTargets, and choosing a card for a cost doesn't target it.

Ability limits

A limit says how often an ability can be used in a period. The builder has two calls for it, both taking a limit object from AbilityLimit.ts (import { perConflict } from '../../AbilityLimit.js').

Unless otherwise specified, each triggered ability can only be used once per game round. This general restriction applies to any triggered ability that does not have "Limit X per [period]" printed as part of the ability’s text.
Limits of Triggered Abilities
This phrase specifies the number of times a triggered ability can be used during the designated period. This replaces the general restriction of using a triggered ability once per game round. Each copy of an ability with a specified limit may be used the specified number of times during the specified period. If a card leaves play and re-enters play during the same period, or if a card transitions from one out-of-play area to another (such as going from hand to discard pile), it is considered a new instance of the card. There is no memory of having used the ability during the specified period for any new instance of a card. All limits are player specific. If the effects of an ability with a limit are canceled, the use of the ability is still counted against the limit.
Limit X per [period]
This phrase imposes a maximum number of times that an ability may be initiated from all copies (by title) of cards bearing the ability (including itself), during the designated period. Initiating an ability on a card counts toward the maximum for all copies of that card. Each maximum is player specific. If the effects of a card or ability with a maximum are canceled, the use of the card or ability is still counted against the maximum. An ability’s maximum value cannot be modified.
Max X per [period]

  • An ability can have both: Assassination's "(Max 1 per round.)" is .max(perRound(1)) and keeps the default limit of once per round per copy.
  • Each can be called once; a second call throws. A then() step can't have them (setup throws); put them on the ability.
  • Keyword abilities set their own: Pride is once per conflict; Covert, Rally, Tutored, Thriving, Courtesy and Sincerity have no limit.
  • Pass one limit object to several abilities to share it: The Empty City's "You can only trigger one of this card's abilities per round" creates perRound(1) once and gives it to both actions.

When a use is counted

  • A use counts once the costs are paid and the targets chosen, before the effects initiate. Cancelling the ability while paying or choosing doesn't count it; cancelling its effects afterwards does.
  • The limit is not counted for a card in hand (an event, or an ability used from the hand), nor when the source changed location since the ability was initiated (it sacrificed or returned itself as a cost). The max is counted in both cases.
  • An ability resolved by another card's effect (resolveAbility) ignores the limit and doesn't count toward it, but counts toward the max. A sub-resolution (the ability resolving again, mayResolveAgain) counts toward neither and ignores the max.
  • The max is keyed by the card's name and the ability's number on the card: copies of the same card share it, and each player has their own.

Copied and gained abilities

  • An ability gained from an effect (gainAbility, gainAbility.action(…)) without .limit() gets its own once-per-round limit on each card that gains it. Its uses are counted per player and per card that holds it, and carry on when the effect is applied to the same card again.
  • A copied ability (an existing ability passed to gainAbility) gets a fresh copy of the original's limit and max: the same number and period, no uses yet. It keeps the original's ability number, so its max is keyed by the name of the card that now holds it.
  • A gained or copied ability is not printed: effects that raise the limit of printed abilities don't apply to it.
