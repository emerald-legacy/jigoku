Jigoku Card builder reference

Engine internals

How the engine checks and resolves an ability, how engine code runs game actions, how cards listen to events, and where the engine knowingly differs from the rules. Card code rarely needs this page; start at debugging when an ability isn't offered.

How an ability is checked

Before an ability is offered, meetsRequirements(context, ignoredBlockers) returns the first Blocker that stops it, or Blocker.None. Each class below runs its own checks, then its base class's.

  1. The action (CardAction):
    1. WrongLocation: the card is where the ability works.
    2. WrongProvince: a province's action needs a conflict at a province passing conflictProvinceCondition (see below).
    3. WrongPhase: the ability's phase(), plus the Dynasty phase's rules on usable actions.
    4. WrongPlayer: the card's controller, unless anyPlayer() or an effect lets the opponent trigger it. Not checked for events.
    5. ConditionNotMet: the ability's condition().
    A TriggeredAbility checks only WrongPlayer and ConditionNotMet here; it listens only while its card is in one of its locations, and its when() is checked when the event happens. Opponents never trigger forced abilities, even with an effect allowing them to trigger the card. An event passes WrongPlayer for any player who can play it from where it is.
  2. The card ability (CardAbility):
    1. Blanked: a printed ability on a blank card, or a gained one while the card loses gained abilities.
    2. CannotTrigger (triggered abilities only): the card is facedown, or a restriction stops it triggering. Ignoring CannotTrigger skips only the restrictions.
    3. CannotPlay: an event that can't be played (ignored when another ability resolves it).
    4. ConditionNotMet: an event's "Play only if" (meetsPlayConditions) fails; this check can't be skipped.
    5. IfConditionNotMet: the ability's ifCondition() is false.
    6. CannotInitiate: a keyword ability while keywords can't be initiated.
    7. LimitReached, then MaxReached: the ability's limit() and max().
    8. LimitedAlreadyPlayed: a Limited card when one was played this round.
    9. WrongPhase: a dynasty event outside the Dynasty phase.
  3. The ability (BaseAbility):
    1. CannotPayCost: some cost can't be paid.
    2. Without targets: ConditionNotMet when none of its game actions has a legal target, unless all are optional and its then step can resolve (ResolvableAbility.checkGameActionsForPotential). A handler-only ability passes. With targets: NoLegalTarget when a target has no legal choice.
    3. A card target without its own game action, whose effect is the ability's (.target({ … }).bow()), offers only cards for which one of the ability's game actions has a legal target (RRG: an ability needs the potential to change the game state; Wildfire Kick can't be played with no defenders). This applies only to a single-card target that is the ability's only target, with no dependent target and no following step (hasFollowingStep()), since an action may read a later target and a step may change the game on its own.

The province check:

card.type !== CardType.Province            // other cards always pass
|| canTriggerOutsideConflict               // canTriggerOutsideConflict()
|| currentConflict.getConflictProvinces().some((province) => conflictProvinceCondition(province, context))
// the default conflictProvinceCondition is province === this card

Ignoring blockers

resolveAbility and the play actions pass ignoredBlockers to skip checks. Only these can be skipped:

BlockerSkips
WrongLocationWhere the card is.
WrongProvinceThe province check (Keeper of Secret Names resolves a province's action this way).
WrongPhaseAll three phase checks.
WrongPlayerWho may use the ability.
ConditionNotMetThe ability's condition(); not the check that a game action has a legal target.
LimitReached, MaxReachedThe limit and the max.
CannotPayCostWhether the costs can be paid; resolveAbility then also skips paying them.
CannotTriggerRestrictions on triggering the card's abilities (not a facedown card).
CannotPlayWhether an event can be played.
IfConditionNotMetThe ability's ifCondition() (Countryside Trader, The Mirror's Gaze).
LimitedAlreadyPlayedThe Limited rule, in card abilities; play actions always check it.
FacedownThat the card is facedown. Checked only by the play actions (dynasty character from a province, Disguised, duplicate onto a unique character). PlayDisguisedCharacterAsIfFromHand with facedown: true skips it (Third Whisker Warrens, top of the dynasty deck).

resolveAbility always ignores WrongPlayer, WrongLocation, LimitReached, CannotTrigger, CannotPlay and LimitedAlreadyPlayed (resolving isn't playing); a sub-resolution also ignores MaxReached.

Never skippable: Blanked, CannotInitiate, NoLegalTarget, and CannotTrigger for a facedown card. The play actions report a play restriction as CannotPlay, like events. Only playing a card raises DuplicateUnique, EnterPlayRestricted (checks the player and, for every play action, the card: Legendary enters play only in the Dynasty phase), CannotEnterConflict (a conflict-only play whose character can't go in; never falls back to home) and CannotPlaceFate.

Debugging an ability that isn't offered

An action window offers a card's actions whose meetsRequirements returns Blocker.None (ActionWindow.onCardClicked); with none, the click does nothing. Log the blockers in a spec:

const card = this.player1.findCardByName('assassination');
console.log(card.getActions().map((action) => `${action.title}: ${action.meetsRequirements(action.createContext(this.player1.player))}`));
// [ 'Discard a character: condition' ] (before a conflict)

The values are the Blocker strings ('condition' is ConditionNotMet, '' is None); look the blocker up in the list above. Other causes:

  • The ability isn't listed: the card has no class under that id (check static id against the card data), or the ability is a reaction or interrupt (not in getActions()). A card in hand also lists its play actions.
  • 'target': no card passes the target. The target's controller defaults to Players.Any, and its game action must be able to affect the card (honoring an honored character can't).
  • A reaction or interrupt: it listens only while its card is in its location() (by default the play area; a hand for events, the provinces for holdings and provinces), its when() key must be the event's name with a callback returning true for that event (events), and then the blockers above are checked with that event in the context (TriggeredAbility.eventHandler).

How an ability resolves

AbilityResolver runs these steps:

  1. createSnapshot(): a character, holding or attachment source's state goes into context.cardStateWhenInitiated (not for events).
  2. resolveEarlyTargets(): unless cannotTargetFirst(), targets are offered before costs at Stage.PreTarget, with "Pay costs first" unless a cost depends on a target (targetDependentFate when an attachment is played).
  3. checkForCancel().
  4. openInitiateAbilityEventWindow(): one window with onCardAbilityInitiated (onAbilityResolverInitiated if !isCardAbility() or isStep()), plus onCardPlayed for a played card and onCardAbilityTriggered when isTriggeredAbility() (actions too). The initiate event's handler queues:
    1. resolveCosts(), payCosts() (one event window), checkCostsWerePaid() (a cancelled cost calls stopForUnpaidCosts(), below);
    2. resolveTargets() at Stage.Target, then checkForCancel();
    3. initiateAbilityEffects():
      • checks the costs of targeting (effects that make choosing a card cost fate) can be paid, else stopForUnpaidCosts();
      • counts the limit (not for a card in hand or one that changed location since it was used) and the max, writes the chat line, pays the costs of targeting;
      • if isTriggeredAbility(): moves a played event to "being played" (controlled by the player playing it, even an opponent's card, until it goes to its owner's discard pile) and opens a then-window with onInitiateAbilityEffects. Cancelling that event cancels the effects; costs stay paid, the limit stays counted. Other abilities go straight on.
    4. executeHandler() at Stage.Effect: the game actions resolve.
    5. moveEventCardToDiscard().
  5. refillProvinces().

stopForUnpaidCosts() is the only way an ability stops for costs (also in ResolveAbilityActionResolver). It writes "did not successfully pay the required costs", sends a played event whose costs were partly paid to its discard pile, raises onCardPlayCostsNotPaid with { playEvent } for a played card, and cancels the initiate events. An event that stops after its costs were paid (a cost was prevented, or no legal target is left) also goes to its discard pile (RRG Event); one cancelled before any cost was paid stays in hand.

An ability resolved by another one (resolveAbility) runs ResolveAbilityActionResolver:

  • No early targets and no play or trigger costs. Its own costs and the costs of targeting are paid unless CannotPayCost is ignored, and only those decide whether it can resolve (an event's fate cost never blocks it).
  • onCardAbilityInitiated with resolvedWithoutTriggering: true in a normal window (plus onCardAbilityTriggered with the flag, except for a sub-resolution), then onInitiateAbilityEffects with the flag in a then-window, so reactions to the effects (Supporting Cast) come after them.
  • Counts the max (not for a sub-resolution), never the limit.

Other details:

  • A reaction to onCardAbilityInitiated fires after the early targets, so a select before the dash is answered before the reaction's window opens.
  • In the interrupt window when a card is played, its player gets no Pass button while they still need a cost reduction (getMinCostReduction() > 0); the opponent always does.
  • isTriggeredAbility(): true for every card ability, actions included; false for keyword abilities (Pride, Rally, Covert, Tutored, Thriving, Courtesy, Sincerity), steps and a bare BaseAbility. A reaction filtering on event.ability.isTriggeredAbility() therefore also reacts to actions.
  • isTriggeredAbilityInstance(): true only for a TriggeredAbility (reactions, interrupts, forced ones, duel triggers, and the keywords built on it: Pride, Rally, Tutored, Thriving, Courtesy, Sincerity; not Covert).
  • isCardAbility(): true for every ResolvableAbility (card abilities, keyword abilities, steps) and Covert. A step (after then()/afterwards()) runs its own AbilityResolver, has isStep(), raises onAbilityResolverInitiated instead of onCardAbilityInitiated, and ends silently (no "insufficient legal targets" line) without a legal target.

Rule steps worth knowing

  • Illegal participants (Conflict.checkForIllegalParticipants(), RRG Dash / Participating): a participant that can no longer take part (a dash in the conflict's new type, or a restriction) is placed at home bowed. It is a rule, not a card effect, so "cannot be moved/sent home" (Dead Eyes) doesn't stop it.
  • Taking a ring's fate: claiming or taking a ring, declaring a conflict at it and switching the contested ring all use takeAllFateFromRing(ring, context) (TakeFateFromRingAction.ts). It returns the takeFateFromRing events (none if the player can't take fate from rings) and adds the "takes N fate" line; the fate moves in a then-window right after the claim or switch.
  • Hantei XXXVIII's blind choice (ruling #926): when he takes over a choice and every card on offer is in the other player's hand or deck (isBlindChoice, GameActions/blindChoice.ts), he chooses at random (chooseBlindly, selectBlindly), or nothing if the cards need a cardCondition. Used by card targets, select card actions and costs, chosen discards, chosen returns to deck and deck searches.
  • Fate costs to attack a province (Foothills Keep): the province can be declared against only if the fate can be paid to another unclaimed ring; it is paid with a cost context, and the conflict fails to initiate if it isn't.
  • Costs to declare (ConflictFlow.payDeclarationCosts, DeclarationCost in gamesteps/conflict/declarationCosts.ts): once choices are made, the attackers' fate, honor and card costs, costToDeclareAnyParticipants and the province's fate to a ring (or the defenders' honor and participant costs) are paid in one window. A DeclarationCost is { costs, paidMessages?, failed? }. paidMessages(events) gets the uncancelled events; its lines follow the window (none if all were cancelled). If a cost with failed wasn't paid (no event, or cancelled), failed is called instead and stops the declaration (for the province's fate: the conflict fails to initiate).

Running game actions from engine code

A game action first collects its events with action.addEventsToArray(events, context), then an event window resolves them. Most actions add one event per legal target; a card game action adds one per target and the window cancels those whose target it can't affect (unnamed events skip that check). Open the window one step later, as the entry points below do: some actions add their events only after a prompt.

Contexts for engine code:

  • Acting for a player outside any ability: game.getFrameworkContext(player). There is no player-less context: a rule acts for the controller of the card it affects (a ring's claimant); the glory count's Imperial Favor claim acts for the winner.
  • A cost the rules charge (honor to declare an attacker or defender): game.getFrameworkCostContext(player), a framework context at Stage.Cost, so "cannot lose honor to pay costs" (Kuni Juurou) stops it and effect-only restrictions don't. Declaring is refused when it can't be paid; for attackers the cost is summed.
  • A step affecting both players' cards (bowing illegal participants, readying, removing fate in the fate phase, returning rings): game.resolveRuleAction(action, [{ target, player }]), one context per player in one window, so reactions such as Ready for Battle never see it as the opponent's doing.
  • A lasting effect acts for its creator (controller in its properties: the ability's player); so does a persistent effect whose source has no controller (a ring, the framework).
Entry pointUse
game.resolveActions([bow({ target }), dishonor({ target })], context)Several actions in one window, then emptied provinces refill; returns the events. For rules, rings, keywords, prompts, and handlers resolving simultaneous things. Context required.
action.resolve(target, context)One action inside an ability, such as in a handler; the ability refills at its end.
game.openEventWindow(events)Events already collected (costs, contingent events).
game.openThenEventWindow(events)Events resolved while another event window is open; their reactions wait for that window. With no window open, it opens a normal one.
game.raiseEvent(name, params, handler)A framework event without a game action.

Imports and cycles

  • Engine code imports the factories by name. Files whose own names clash with them (the costs, AbilityBuilder) use import * as GameActions.
  • The modules in GameActions/ and AbilityResolver construct the action classes (new MoveCardAction(…)): the factory module imports them, so importing it back creates a cycle that breaks depending on load order.
  • A class must not share an import cycle with its base class (DrawCard extends BaseCard needs BaseCard loaded): nothing that BaseCard, CardAbility or ReduceableFateCost imports may import their subclasses as values.
  • Tell cards and abilities apart with the guards (isCard(), isDrawCard(), isRing(), isPlayer(), isToken(), isTriggeredAbilityInstance(); value instanceof GameObject && value.isCard() for an unknown), not instanceof on the class.
  • The modules in GameActions/ import their effects from Effects/Library/, not the effects index, which pulls in the ability builder.

Listening to events: EventRegistrar

A card that tracks something across events (characters bowed this conflict, provinces attacked) listens with an EventRegistrar, whose listeners last the whole game:

  • register({ [EventName.X]: handler }) runs after the event, with the event typed by its name. It also sees events that open no window (onCardMoved, onDeckShuffled).
  • registerTriggerWindow(eventName, abilityType, handler) runs in the event's trigger window of that type, before its abilities are offered.
  • unregisterAll() removes the listeners (the duel uses it for its own registrar).
// Kakita's First Kata: Conflict Action: Choose a [clan-crane] or Duelist character you control – until the end of the conflict, that character cannot be bowed or moved by opponents' card effects. If it was bowed during this conflict, ready it.
this.eventRegistrar = new EventRegistrar(this.game);
this.eventRegistrar.register({
    [EventName.OnConflictFinished]: () => this.onConflictFinished(),
    [EventName.OnCardBowed]: (event) => this.onCardBowed(event)
});
// Pilgrimage: During conflicts at this province, cancel all ring effects.
const eventRegistrar = new EventRegistrar(this.game);
eventRegistrar.registerTriggerWindow(EventName.OnResolveRingElement, AbilityType.WouldInterrupt, (event) => this.cancelRingEffect(event));

A listener bypasses the ability system (no costs, limits, chat or restrictions); game changes still go through game actions (game.resolveActions).

Technical Limitations And Known Issues

Deliberate deviations from the rules, where a fix would cost more than the case is worth.

"Characters you control" is read as "characters on your side"

Cards say either "on your side" / "the enemy side" or "you control" / "your opponent controls". The engine reads both as the side: conflict.getCharacters(player), hasMoreParticipants and getNumberOfParticipantsFor all go by side.

  • This matters only for a character on its opponent's side (Endless Plains Skirmisher: "… on that player's side. You still control this character"; Join the Fray). Then Misinformation ("each participating character your opponent controls") misses it, and "if you control more participating characters" counts it for the wrong player.
  • Affected wording: Arrows from the Woods, Void Fist, Ramparts of Stone, Wildfire Kick, Warrior Poet, Fearsome Mystic, Isawa Atsuko, Capture the False Eye, Misinformation, Shinjo Shono, Menacing Iron Warrior, Subdue the Spirits, Overhear, Show Me Your Stance; the counts of Press of Battle, Even the Odds, Flank the Enemy, Moment of Perfect Beauty.
  • Not fixed: putting a character on the opponent's side is rare (mostly multiplayer, where Join the Fray targets you or your partner).

Copies read the original card's element symbols

About 45 cards with an element symbol in their text (Isawa Skycaller, Kitsuki Seiji, Courteous Greeting, the province-element cards) read it with this.getCurrentElementSymbol(key) or helpers such as claimsRingOf(this, key, event), i.e. from the card that wrote the ability. The symbols are a per-class field (printedElementSymbols), so a copy (Shosuro Actor, Illusionary Terrain) or a card that gained the ability has none of its own.

  • This matters only after Twin Soul Temple changed the original's symbol: the copy reads the changed element, and Twin Soul Temple can't choose the copy's symbol. An original out of play (copied from a discard pile by Floating Fortress or Spirit of Valor) keeps its printed symbols.
  • Not fixed: own symbols for copies would change the element-symbol model for a rare combination.

A province turned facedown keeps its attachments

Turning a province facedown makes it leave play (RRG Facedown Province; rulings #248, #606, #986), and a card leaving play discards its attachments. TurnFacedownAction treats the province as leaving play (its lasting effects end), but attachments on it stay attached.

  • Affected: Makeshift War Camp and Inventive Buttressing on a province that Saadiyah al-Mozedu or Kitsuki Yaruma turns facedown; the attachment applies again when the province is revealed.
  • Not fixed.

Facedown cards in the stronghold province are revealed last

RRG 1.2 "Reveal facedown dynasty cards" turns the stronghold province's cards faceup first, then the other provinces left to right. DynastyPhase follows game.getProvinceArray(), which lists the stronghold province last.

  • Only the order of onCardRevealed events and their reactions (Rally) differs; every card is still revealed before onRevealFacedownDynastyCards.
  • Affected: a facedown card moved into the stronghold province by an effect (Recalled Defenses).
  • Not fixed.
